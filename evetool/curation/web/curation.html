How to curate

Untick whatever is a false positive, then use the buttons to export a FASTA of the approved EVEs only, run the hierarchical clustering or draw the genomic-context figures. Candidates found by the HMM alone appear in group 5_HMM_ONLY and can be selected like any other. The selection lives in curation_selection.json and does not alter the pipeline tables. Synteny is optional: tick the box before clustering across species, or use the synteny column in the species TSV to control it row by row. Rows marked review hit a viral target whose genome type is unknown in RefSeq: decide them here like any other row. Every chart follows the approved EVEs and the manual family/protein, and the page refreshes itself when the data on disk changes.

How it works · what it means

Click a slice or a bar to filter the table. "HMM domain only" are the segments with no alignment that only the HMM supports. CD-Search is the conserved-domain search on CDD (local rpsblast+ by default); its domains are placed in a scope from their CDD description and source taxon, and only RNA virus and ssDNA virus (non-retroviral) count as in scope — reverse transcriptases, integrases, gag and pararetroviruses are kept apart. Pfam (hmmscan on Pfam-A with the Pfam gathering thresholds) uses the same scopes; every source is drawn on the ORF under inspect.

Repeats and low complexity around the EVEs

How it works · what it means

Each approved EVE locus (overlapping ORFs of one insertion together) gets three checks. Low complexity: dustmasker on the EVE sequence and segmasker on its peptide; a candidate mostly masked (≥ 50%) is probably a compositional artefact, not a viral fragment. Soft-masking: NCBI assemblies write repeats in lower case, so the masked share of the EVE and of each flank is compared with the whole genome. Transposons nearby: the ORFs of the flanks (six frames, ≥ 100 aa) are searched with the transposon domains of Pfam-A (gathering cut-offs) and grouped into LTR retrotransposon (integrase, gag, protease), retrotransposon RT, non-LTR endonuclease, DNA transposon and Helitron. Each locus then reads low complexity, inside TE (a TE domain overlaps the EVE, or it is mostly masked), TE nearby (a TE domain within the flank), repeat-rich flank (flanks masked well above the genome), clean, or short flanks when the contig leaves nothing to judge. Non-retroviral EVEs often sit next to retrotransposons, whose reverse transcriptase may have copied the viral RNA into DNA. Runs on segments use the whole genomes of synteny_genomes.tsv.

EVEs on the viral proteins

How it works · what it means

Each approved EVE realigned (Smith-Waterman, BLOSUM62) on a viral reference protein, the grey bar. With shared, the EVEs of one family go on the protein most of them hit in the DIAMOND search, so the fragments of one gene line up; * marks the EVEs whose own best hit is that protein. Each row is a genomic-context cluster, so the pieces of one broken gene share a row. Hatched bars align on the opposite strand or in another frame than the ORF (blastx found the viral signal there), so the ORF itself is probably not the viral reading frame. Every approved ORF is drawn, also the overlapping ones hidden by "one row per locus". Click a bar to inspect the EVE.

Online BLASTX (NCBI)

      
How it works · what it means
Sends the genome sequence of the chosen segments to NCBI BLASTX in batches of at most
        20 sequences and 10,000 nt (longer segments are split into overlapping windows). Each batch keeps
        its RID on disk, so the run survives a closed page or a restarted server: press Resume.
        Segments already searched with the same database and E-value come from the cache.
        The NCBI queue can take from minutes to hours.

How it works · what it means
Approved EVEs grouped by where they sit in the genome.

        rejecting an EVE removes it here, so a pipeline cluster can turn into a singleton.

tblastn scan of the viral references

        
        
        
  
        
        
        
        
      

      
How it works · what it means
The best viral hit of every ORF kept by the DIAMOND
        screening is aligned back on the whole genome with tblastn (six frames, no ORF needed), so
        viral stretches broken by stops or frameshifts show up too. HSPs closer than the distance above
        form a segment; every orfipy ORF inside it is listed, the candidates and the ORFs never
        analysed (new), with their screening result and their CD-Search and Pfam domains.
        The ORFs come from the scan's own distance: to characterise the ORFs between HSPs that only a
        larger distance joins, run the scan again with that distance. Expand a segment for its map and alignments.

      

        
        
        
        
      

      

      

        
        
        
        
        
        
      

      

    

  

  

  

    
EVE clusters shared between species

      
      
      
      
      
      
      
      
    

    
How it works · what it means
The approved EVEs of every
      species are aligned all-vs-all with tblastx and clustered in two stages (pairs above the identity and
      coverage of the run become edges; each group is then split at the valleys of its distance distribution).
      A cluster with segments of two or more species is shared, but only a pair of segments of
      different species that align well (identity and coverage above the values here) says the
      insertion may be the same — an orthologous EVE or a very close viral source. Shared clusters without
      such a pair are distant: the same viral family, not the same insertion, and they stay out unless
      show distant is ticked. Species and clusters are ordered by average-linkage trees over which
      clusters each species carries (Jaccard), so species with the same EVEs sit side by side. Click a
      cluster for the tblastx identity and coverage of every pair of its segments, ordered by their
      distance (1 − bitscore of the pair / bitscore of the self-hit).


      Units: the comparison can use each approved ORF, each genomic-context cluster or each tblastn segment,
      the last two merged at the distance chosen here. Flank synteny: for every locus of a shared cluster the
      flanks are read from its genome and their host genes are labelled with DIAMOND blastx against the reference
      proteome. A pair of loci of different species is syntenic when they share neighbour genes on both
      sides (either orientation), one side when only one side matches, not syntenic when both have
      neighbours and none is shared, and insufficient when a flank has no labelled gene (short contig).
      Syntenic loci are probably one ancestral insertion; not syntenic ones, independent integrations.
      Genes found next to more distinct sites than max sites in one species, and mobile elements, are
      ignored. Runs made on pre-extracted segments use the whole genomes listed in
      synteny_genomes.tsv in the results folder.
