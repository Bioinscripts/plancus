How to read a card

One card per EVE locus (the overlapping ORFs of one insertion together, shown through their representative ORF). The header carries the viral family and protein, the length, the verdict and the read evidence of every library mapped on the page: DNA = libraries where the insertion is present / libraries with data; RNA = RNA-seq libraries with ≥ 5 reads inside the locus; sRNA = small RNA reads and the strongest piRNA signature (● ping-pong, ▲ primary 5′U, ▼ A10 only). Opening a card reads the locus from the server: verdict, the genomic context with every read track, the domain architecture, the reads library by library, the tblastn segments and the clusters it belongs to, and its sequence. Curating (qualify / reject, family, protein) stays in Inspection: the button at the end of each card opens it.

Genomic DNA is the insertion in the genome of each library: fixed, polymorphic or absent

Presence in the DNA libraries

How it works · what it means

Each row is an approved EVE locus, each column a DNA library (the assembly reads first, then the individuals and pools by population). The call is the one of the Genomic DNA tab, with its thresholds: present = the EVE is covered and its depth matches the flanks; het = present, with reads of a chromosome without the insertion as well (reads clipped at the junction); partial = only part of the EVE covered or its depth well below the flanks; absent = no read on the EVE while the flanks are covered; excess = much deeper than the flanks or the genome (a repeat, or a copy elsewhere landing here); no data = the flanks have too few reads to say. A fixed insertion is present in every individual; a polymorphic one in some. Libraries mapped elsewhere (a cluster) enter with their measurements only. Hover a cell for its numbers; click a row to open its card.

RNA-seq transcription of each EVE, library by library

Transcription in the RNA-seq libraries

How it works · what it means

Reads of every RNA-seq BAM of the species (short reads, long reads, Iso-Seq) counted over each approved locus: reads inside the EVE have aligned bases in it (a read whose splice gap only jumps over the EVE is not counted), mean depth is averaged over the locus, share covered is the fraction with at least one read. The colour is on a log scale, one hue, darker = more; empty = no read. For a stranded library the hover gives the depth on each genome strand. A transcribed EVE is not proof of a functional gene: read-through from a host gene or a nearby transposon transcribes it as well — the card shows whether the reads continue into the flanks.

Small RNA reads

piRNA calls:
How it works · what it means

Small RNA reads mapped with the sRNAProfiler method (Bowtie1, at most one mismatch); a read placed at N sites counts 1/N at each, and a read belongs to an EVE when at least half of it falls inside. The cell colour is the abundance (log scale); the glyph is the piRNA signature of the piRNA-sized reads (24–31 nt), read strand by strand: ▲ primary — one strand carries a 5′ uridine bias (5′ U at or above the threshold), the mark of primary piRNAs cut from long precursor transcripts (piRNA clusters); EVE-derived primary piRNAs are usually antisense to the viral ORF. ▼ secondary — an adenine at position 10 (A10) on a strand, the mark of secondary piRNAs, without a significant ping-pong overlap. ● ping-pong — the 5′ ends of piRNAs on opposite strands overlap by exactly 10 nt more often than by any other distance (z-score of the 10-nt overlap against 1–20 nt, sRNAProfiler); with a 5′ U strand and an A10 strand facing each other this is the ping-pong amplification cycle: the primary piRNA guides the cleavage of a complementary transcript, whose fragment becomes the secondary piRNA. ◆ siRNA — sRNAProfiler calls the locus siRNA (20–22 nt, both strands, no ping-pong). The thresholds above change the calls at once; the table under the heatmap lists every locus with enough piRNA-sized reads, strand by strand (sense / antisense = relative to the EVE's ORF).
